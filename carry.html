The five things that break together

An unwind isn't just the basket losing money. It's losses arriving fast, funders surging, correlations converging, and volatility expanding at the same moment. Each is scored against its own past year.

Where the pressure is

Moves are shown as appreciation against the dollar. In an unwind, the funders at the top turn green and the targets below turn red.

What this can and can't see. It reads spot rates only, so it tracks the price leg of the trade and not the interest you'd actually earn. A carry position with a flat spot line is still making money. That's deliberate — the interest accrues smoothly and tells you nothing about stress, while the violence always shows up in spot.

It is coincident, not leading. These measures confirm an unwind that is already underway, usually within a day or two of it starting. Nothing here gives advance warning. What would is positioning data — CFTC Commitments of Traders, published Fridays — and option-implied volatility, particularly risk reversals on yen crosses, where the cost of downside protection rises before spot moves. Neither is available free in a form a page like this can fetch.

The basket is an assumption, not a measurement. Which currencies fund and which are targets depends on policy rates this page cannot see, so the split is hard-coded and will drift out of date as central banks move. Check it against current rates occasionally. Weights are inverse-volatility, so no single emerging market currency dominates the reading.

The marked date is 5 August 2024, the yen carry unwind, kept as a calibration point for what a real one looks like on these scales. Informational only, and not advice about positioning.
