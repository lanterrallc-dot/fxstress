Rates are the European Central Bank's daily reference fixings, published once each business day at about 16:00 Central European Time, via the free Frankfurter API. The page re-checks every 15 minutes, so it stays current with the feed — but the feed itself is daily, not tick-by-tick. Weekends and holidays show Friday's reading.

Positioning comes from the CFTC's Commitments of Traders report, published each Friday afternoon describing where large speculators stood the preceding Tuesday. It is never less than three days old, it covers only the seven currencies with liquid futures contracts rather than all twenty-one above, and it is a stock rather than a flow. It is deliberately kept out of the composite for those reasons — mixing a stale weekly series into a daily index would change what the whole year of history above means.

This is a rough gauge built from spot rates and futures positioning. Professional stress measures also read option-implied volatility, cross-currency basis, and funding spreads, none of which are free — the one public series that came close, the CBOE EuroCurrency index, stopped updating in March 2025. Treat all of it as a thermometer, not a trading signal.
